# Comparison Report Format

Section layout for Step 7 of [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md). Read it before writing the report.

**Money in every section**: lead every amount with the user's currency, in tables and sentences alike, including prices quoted from foreign sources and costs mentioned in passing. Never write a foreign amount alone, even when quoting a source: write "the price rose from ₱2,800 to ₱3,900 (US\$50 to US\$70)", not "rose from US\$50 to US\$70". If no current exchange rate was found, say so once above the first table and mark converted amounts approximate.

## 1. Verdict & Bottom Line

- **Verdict**: the user-facing label of the overall verdict (the **Show the user** column of the overall verdict table in `SKILL.md`).
- **Scorecard**: each option's categories won (clear wins, slight edges, ties) next to its overall score out of 10.
- **Bottom line**: 2 to 3 decisive sentences on why the winner won (or why it is too close to call). When the winner took fewer categories than the runner-up, explain in one plain sentence that it won the categories that matter most to the user.

## 2. Head-to-Head Scoreboard

- One Markdown table with columns: `Category (how much it matters %)` | `Option A (score / 10)` | `Option B (score / 10)` | `[Option C...]` | `Winner`.
- Bottom rows: **Categories won** and **Overall score (out of 10)**.

## 3. Category by Category

For each category: how much it matters (%), the like-for-like evidence (with dated, session-verified sources), whether the user will notice the difference day to day, each option's score out of 10, and the result (clear win, slight edge, or tie) with its reason.

## 4. What Only One Option Has, and the Core Trade-off

Unique strengths, structural differences, or one-sided risks that do not fit the table, followed by the core trade-off between the top two options in plain units (for example, *"Choosing A over B costs about [amount] more per year and gets you Y"*).

## 5. Who Should Pick What, and What Would Change the Winner

- **Pick by situation**: concise bullet rules (`Choose [Option A] if...`, `Choose [Option B] if...`).
- **What would change the winner**: exact numbers or conditions that would make the runner-up come out on top (for example, *"B wins instead if price matters more to you than battery life, if B drops below [price], or if you need more than Z users"*).
