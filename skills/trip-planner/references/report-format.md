# Readiness Report Format

Section layout for Step 6 of [`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md). Read it before writing the final report.

**Money in every section**: lead every cost with the user's home currency, in tables and sentences alike, with the local price after it ("₱7,170 (S\$150)", never "S\$150 (₱7,170)"), including daily costs and taxi or ticket prices mentioned in passing. Never write a local amount alone: write "entry is about ₱1,150 (¥3,000) each", not "entry is about ¥3,000 each". Put the home currency in the first price column of every budget and hotel table. State each exchange rate and its date once above the first table, or say it is approximate.

## 1. Verdict & Bottom Line

- **Verdict**: `[Ready to Book | Viable With Adjustments | Cut Stops / Slow Down | Reroute / Change Base | Shift Season / Dates | Do Not Book As Planned]`
- **Bottom line**: 2 to 3 decisive sentences stating the verdict, the main routing or pacing reason, and any blocker or cost surprise. For an overpacked plan, state "X of about Y waking hours in transit (Z%)" and the total hours lost changing hotels, then name the consolidated alternative and what it gives up.
- **Before-you-go checks**: a table with the status of passport dates, visa or entry permit, driving permit, health and altitude rules, weather, and holidays or closures.
- **Where to stay** (when choosing bases, hotels, or routes): a side-by-side table across Room Rate, Required Fees & Taxes, Daily Getting-Around Cost, Daily Commute Time, Real Cost per Night, and Walkability & Feel.

## 2. Day by Day

- Head each day with **Day N (weekday, date) | where you're staying | area of town**.
- Write **Morning**, **Afternoon**, and **Evening** as three separate lines. Each line gives the main plan with checked hours and last entry, one optional extra, the door-to-door travel leg (how you get there, ride time, extra buffer, what to do with bags), where to eat, and ends with "Plan B: ..." in the same area.

## 3. What Could Go Wrong

- **Connections**: whether each connection is on one booking or separate tickets, the layover against the airport's minimum connection time, and the riskiest points in the trip.
- **Money at risk**: non-refundable bookings, free-cancellation deadlines, and gaps in insurance or card coverage.
- **Likeliest problems**: the 2 to 3 likeliest ways this trip could go wrong and what the plan already does about each.

## 4. What to Book When, and What Would Change the Verdict

- **Booking checklist**: when any before-you-go check is a blocker, the first item fixes it and every non-refundable booking or timed ticket waits until it clears. Then bookings grouped into `90+ Days Out`, `60 to 30 Days Out`, `14 to 7 Days Out`, and `48 Hours / Walk-Up`, each linked to the official booking site.
- **What would change the verdict**: exact conditions, each of which removes the reason for the current verdict (for example, *"Switches from `Cut Stops / Slow Down` to `Ready to Book` if you add 3 nights or visit Nara as a day trip from Kyoto instead of moving hotels"*).

## 5. Save for Next Time (when applicable)

If the session revealed lasting traveler context (passport dates, home airport, daily walking limit, dietary or medical needs, or lessons from this trip), ask in one line whether to save it as the traveler profile for future trips.
